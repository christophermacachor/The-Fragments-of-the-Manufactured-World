I. Scope, Evidence, and Interpretation

This archive brings together reporting, public records, scholarly literature, and the author's cross-domain interpretation. Its central synthesis is an argument to be examined—not a conclusion established merely by repetition across sections.

Evidence key

  • Documented fact: a claim directly supported by an identifiable primary record, peer-reviewed source, official statement, or credible reporting. Where the only available source is an interested party, that limitation must be stated.
  • Reported claim: attributed to a named publication, witness, organization, or official, but not independently verified here.
  • Inference: an interpretation drawn from one or more facts. It may be plausible without being proven by those facts.
  • Hypothesis / metaphor: a conceptual analogy or proposed mechanism requiring separate evidence before it can be treated as a literal causal explanation.
  • Unverified: a specific claim for which the archive does not yet provide enough traceable sourcing. It should not be presented as settled fact.

Important boundary: evidence that defense firms earn revenue during conflict does not, by itself, prove that profit caused a particular war. Evidence that a human approval step exists does not, by itself, prove the human is only a liability shield. Evidence that enteric and cortical nervous systems differ does not establish that the enteric nervous system is a privileged truth-signal or that the cortex is systematically manipulated. Those are interpretive claims in this archive and require independent testing.

Editorial rule: quotations, numbers, legal assertions, allegations about named people, and claims about events in 2026 should be linked to exact sources and dated. Allegations are not findings of guilt. Market movements after an event are correlations unless causal evidence is supplied.

XX. Compression-Engine Test Form for the Invariant

The invariant ("money is substrate → profit mechanism → war tool → humans input → …") is a compression claim: it says a short rule reproduces the structure across many cases. To test that, you need a form that scores compression quality — and that lets the invariant fail.

B.1 The Form

For each case (theater/document), fill one row:

compression_ratio(chars of case record) / (chars of invariant rule)number

B.2 Scoring the Compression

  • Coverage = matches / predicted. High coverage = the rule reproduces many cases.
  • Residual burden = count of rows where residual is substantive. High residual = the compression is lossy — it's a compression, not the structure.
  • Discrimination = rows where alt_explanation fits as well as the invariant. If an alternative fits equally, the invariant has not been shown to be the structure; it's one reading among several.
  • Falsifiability = rows with a stated falsifier that was actually checked. An invariant with no checked falsifiers is untested.
  • Net compression score = coverage − (residual weight) − (discrimination penalty), with weights declared in advance (e.g. 0.5 each).

B.3 The Critical Move: Give the Invariant a Rival

A compression engine is only meaningful against competing compressions. Test the archive's invariant against at least two rivals, e.g.:

  • Rival 1 (institutional-pathology): "captured institutions + incentive misalignment produce harm; no single substrate required."
  • Rival 2 (standard political-economy): "defense procurement and regulatory capture are well-documented; profit–war correlation is real but causal direction is contested."

Score all three on the same form. The invariant "wins" only if it has higher net compression and lower discrimination penalty than the rivals — i.e., it explains at least as much while being no more easily satisfied by alternatives.
