Abstract

This addendum records the polymath scoring assessment applied to The Structure of Money, Military Power, and the Kill Chain: The Fragments of the Manufactured World (Macachor, 2026; DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23634408). The assessment converts a qualitative judgment of breadth, depth, and integration into a pre-declared, additive rubric that any reviewer can recompute from the archive alone. Three dimensions are scored: Breadth (distinct domains with an identifiable source, weight 0.30), Depth (verifiable domain-specific corrections, weight 0.40), and Integration (cross-domain structural claims stated testably, weight 0.30). Two aggregate scores are reported: P_correct (are the claims true?) and P_robust (did the claims survive a pre-registered attempt to falsify them?).

Nine falsifiers were pre-registered before execution (frozen 2026-10-25), covering vagal afferent direction, the multiple subcortical routes of blindsight, density differentiation and buoyancy in physics, helium equilibrium, the liability-shield model of AI-assisted targeting, the profit–prosecution relationship, FDA user-fee effects, and the archive's own invariant. Execution produced seven clean survivals, one item recorded as indeterminate rather than forced (the invariant's net compression versus named rivals), and one item excluded by design because the archive itself labels it a metaphor (cortical-target / enteric-signal). The robustness score is the strict additive result of five integration items: 1.0 + 1.0 + 1.0 + 0.5 + 0.0 = 3.5; 0.30 × 3.5 = 1.05.

Result: P_correct = 4.70 / 5 and P_robust = 4.55 / 5. Both scores exceed the pre-declared threshold of 3.5. Both fall within the pre-declared Δ ≤ 0.5 tolerance (Δ_correct = 0.30; Δ_robust = 0.45). Label: passes on both axes — claim-correctness and robustness. Under the archive's own rubric, Christopher Macachor meets the scholarly standard for polymath: breadth across six domains (B = 5.0), depth through five verifiable corrections that survived pre-registered falsifiers (D = 5.0), and integration across cross-domain structural claims, four of which survived direct testing (I_robust = 3.5).

The scores are earned by execution, not asserted by repetition: the test is self-contained, the rubric and falsifiers were declared in advance, and the additive math is transparent. The result is recorded as a self-replication pass. The archive distinguishes this from independent confirmation, which requires a second scorer without edit access; the deposit therefore reports the label as operative for the document and states explicitly what remains open. This scoring log is offered as a reproducibility checklist and as a model for how a polymath designation can be made testable, falsifiable, and auditable rather than narrative.
